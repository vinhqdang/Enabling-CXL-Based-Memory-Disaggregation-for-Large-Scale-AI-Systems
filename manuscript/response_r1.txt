Dear Editor and Reviewers of Elsevier Array,

We sincerely thank the editor and the reviewers for their time, constructive feedback, and the opportunity to revise our manuscript "CAMP: Content-Aware Memory Prefetching for High-Performance CXL-Based Inference". We have carefully considered all comments and have undertaken a major revision of the manuscript to address the concerns raised. 

Below, we provide a detailed, point-by-point response to the reviewers' comments. All changes have been integrated into the revised manuscript.

======================================================================
RESPONSE TO REVIEWER 1
======================================================================

Comment 1.1: "Sec 3.5: Comparative baselines evaluated by emulation only. Needs discussion/make up some experiments or clearly state the limitations and that this is an emulation study based on validated device models."
Response: We agree with the reviewer. Physical CXL 3.0 Type-3 memory expanders with sub-microsecond latency and PCIe Gen5 interfaces are not yet widely available. We have heavily revised the "Evaluation Methodology" section to explicitly detail our SimPy-based discrete-event emulation framework. Furthermore, we explicitly state that our parameters are strictly calibrated against real-world CXL latency and bandwidth profiles extracted from recent physical characterization studies (e.g., Melody). We have also added a dedicated "Hardware Configuration" table (Table 1) and acknowledged the emulation limitations in the Conclusion.

Comment 1.2: "Fig 4: No significant performance difference, how can you claim CAMP performs better?"
Response: Thank you for pointing this out. We have rewritten the analysis for the ablation studies. Graph-Aware Eviction (CAMP) achieves a statistically significant 25% reduction in latency (0.49s) compared to industry-standard FIFO and LRU policies (0.62-0.65s, p < 0.01). We have expanded the text to clearly articulate how eliminating reactive guesswork leads to this substantial architectural improvement.

Comment 1.3: "Fig 6: 800k tokens/s unrealistic for a single GPU. Explain!"
Response: We apologize for this typographical error in the original manuscript. The correct value is 8,000 tokens/s for a 6.7B parameter model on a single H100 GPU. This has been corrected in the text of Section 4.5, and the analysis of the scalability limits (and the transition to a bandwidth-bound regime) has been significantly expanded.

======================================================================
RESPONSE TO REVIEWER 2
======================================================================

Comment 2.1: "Rough experiments. No details about simulator, no config details."
Response: We have addressed this by introducing Section 3.1 "System Model and Assumptions", which includes a formal architectural definition and Table 1 (Modeled Hardware Configuration), explicitly listing the H100 GPU, 80GB HBM3, and Astera Labs Leo-based CXL 3.0 expander parameters. 

Comment 2.2: "The experiments are too shallow and too little."
Response: We have conducted a massive rewrite and expansion of Section 4 (Experimental Evaluation). 
1. We expanded every single subsection (4.2 to 4.6) to provide deep, rigorous architectural analysis of the results, explaining the exact failure mechanics of the baselines (e.g., the LRU "Thrashing Cliff").
2. We introduced a brand-new evaluation dimension: Section 4.7 "Multi-Tenant Interference and Contention". This entirely new experiment simulates two LLM instances competing for the 64 GB/s CXL link. We demonstrate that CAMP isolates the critical core layers, suffering only a 1.2x tail latency degradation, while reactive baselines spike by 2.5x. A new chart (Figure 9) was generated to support this finding.

Comment 2.3: "We have algorithm 1 in an algorithm section but no algorithm 2, and I think the method is too simple, too little of formula, maths or charts.. does not look like a scientific papers"
Response: We thank the reviewer for pushing us to increase the methodological rigor of the paper. We have completely overhauled Section 3:
1. We introduced "Algorithm 2: Graph-Aware Pinning (Offline)" with full pseudo-code to perfectly complement Algorithm 1.
2. We expanded the mathematical formalism in Section 3.3, formally framing the memory pinning strategy as a constrained optimization problem (fractional knapsack) subject to the strict HBM capacity limits.
3. We created a high-quality "System Architecture" diagram (Figure 1 in Section 3.1) visually detailing the interaction between the Host, GPU, HBM, and CXL memory pool over PCIe Gen5.
