TITLE
Where Should Resident Layers Go? Optimal Placement for Weight Streaming in Offloaded LLM Inference

ABSTRACT (248 words)
Models that exceed GPU memory can be served by keeping some layers resident and streaming the others from host-attached memory, such as page-locked DRAM or a CXL expander, through a ring of GPU buffers. Where should the resident layers sit? We model the pipeline as a max-plus linear system whose iteration time is the maximum mean of a family of tilings of the layer sequence. For L layers of equal compute time c and copy time f , r resident, m streamed and k ≤ m buffers we prove three results: resident layers are useless when a layer computes at least as long as it copies; otherwise, spreading the resident layers evenly attains the lower bound max(Lc, m f ) whenever (k − 1) f ≥ 2c, so three buffers suffice; and a contiguous block of resident layers, as produced by model-order filling, takes max(Lc, m f, rc + 2c + (m − k) f ), up to 2 − c/ f times the optimum. On a Tesla T4 with page-locked copies at 12.3 GB/s, similar to current CXL expanders, real Llama-3.2-3B and Llama-3.1-8B layer kernels and per-layer times measured on that GPU, the model reproduces 605 measured iteration times with a mean error of 0.29% and orders 98.6% of distinguishable placement pairs correctly. Even spreading is up to 1.50× faster than a contiguous block and reaches the bound with two buffers where the block needs five. Because the T4 is power-limited, the main measurements lock its clock.
