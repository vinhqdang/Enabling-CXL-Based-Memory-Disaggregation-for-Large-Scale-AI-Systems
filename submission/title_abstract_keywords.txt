TITLE
Streaming LLM Weights from CXL Memory: A Simulation Study of Regimes, Placement and Pipeline-Aware Pinning

ABSTRACT (259 words)
Models larger than GPU memory can be served by streaming weights from CXL memory. The GPU cannot address a CXL expander, so every transfer is a host-initiated DMA limited by the slower of the device and the PCIe link (10–20 GB/s). This is a simulation study: the link is modelled from published measurements, and only the simulator’s overlap logic is checked on a real GPU with pinned host memory as the pool; no CXL device was measured. First, streaming is nearly free only above about 16k tokens per iteration: at 18 GB/s a 70B model is 1.10× slower than resident weights at 16k tokens and 91.7× slower at one token. Second, where resident layers sit matters more than which ones: contiguous resident sets idle the link when compute and transfer are comparable, while interleaved placements, such as a FlexGen-style per-layer split, stay within about 7% of a lower bound. Third, CAMP combines a work-conserving prefetcher with stall-cost-aware pinning (SCP), a plan-and-verify choice of resident layers. On Llama-2/3 (up to 405B) and Gemma-2 CAMP is 1.05–2.82× faster than demand paging and within 1.014–1.069× of the lower bound without noise. Against a tuned per-layer split it is ahead by up to 7%, behind by at most 3.2%, ties on uniform dense models and wins where reuse or unit sizes are non-uniform (up to 1.97× on encoder–decoder inference). Fourth, sharing one expander among G GPUs raises the threshold G-fold, giving a simple sizing rule. The compute-time estimate changes latency by under 1% once calibrated.
