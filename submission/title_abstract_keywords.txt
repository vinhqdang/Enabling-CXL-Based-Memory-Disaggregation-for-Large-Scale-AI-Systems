TITLE
Streaming LLM Weights from CXL Memory: Regimes, Placement and Pipeline-Aware Pinning

ABSTRACT (249 words)
Models whose weights exceed GPU memory can be served by streaming part of the weights from CXL-attached memory. A CXL Type-3 expander is not addressable by the GPU, so every transfer is a host-initiated DMA limited by the slower of the CXL device and the PCIe link (10–20 GB/s). We study this path with an event-driven simulator whose link and overlap logic is checked on a real GPU (pinned host memory replaces the pool; no CXL device was measured). First, streaming is nearly free only above about 16k tokens per iteration: at 18 GB/s a 70B model is 1.10× slower than resident weights at 16k tokens and 91.7× slower at one token. Second, where resident layers sit matters more than which ones: contiguous resident sets idle the link when compute and transfer are comparable, while interleaved placements, such as a FlexGen-style per-layer split, stay within about 7% of an implementation-independent lower bound. Third, we present CAMP, a runtime policy combining a work-conserving prefetcher with stall-cost-aware pinning (SCP), a plan-and-verify choice of resident layers. On Llama-2/3 (up to 405B) and Gemma-2 configurations CAMP is 1.05–2.82× faster than demand paging and within 1.014–1.069× of the lower bound without noise. Against the strongest baseline, a tuned per-layer split, it is ahead by up to 7% and behind by at most 3.2%: it ties on uniform dense models and wins where reuse or unit sizes are non-uniform (up to 1.97× on encoder–decoder inference). The compute-time estimate changes latency by under 1% once calibrated.

KEYWORDS
Compute Express Link; memory disaggregation; LLM inference; prefetching; weight offloading; discrete-event simulation
